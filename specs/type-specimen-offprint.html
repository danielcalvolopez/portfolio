Case-study front matter

Abstract

RetryFi is a failed-payment recovery service for Stripe businesses. I built it alone: architecture, dunning engine, dashboard, OAuth production verification, and launch. This case study covers the design decisions, the numbers behind them, and what I would change.

Body, with a real excerpt

Context

RetryFi exists because involuntary churn is boring and expensive. When a card fails, most SaaS businesses send one badly timed email and give up. The revenue is recoverable, but only if retries are scheduled around the reasons cards actually decline. I wanted the whole problem: product, architecture, infrastructure, and go-to-market, with no one else to blame.

Why server-driven dunning

The dunning engine is server-driven by design. Retry schedules live in Postgres as data, not code, so a schedule change is a migration I can test rather than a deploy I have to fear. Stripe webhooks land in an idempotent queue (Fig. 1), and every state transition is written twice: once for the machine, once for a timeline a support human can read. Warning · What I'd do differently

I shipped the first dunning schedule from intuition instead of decline-code data. It worked, but I could not say why for three weeks, and a schedule I cannot explain is a schedule I cannot defend. The data model now leads; my hunches follow.
